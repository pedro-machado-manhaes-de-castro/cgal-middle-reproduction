Delaunay policy comparison: measurement protocol

The experiment compares original middle, corrected middle and median in CGAL Delaunay_triangulation_2 at commit 0490d81af4220f33005914676f362f349933fbc1. The two executables use the same base and differ only in the supplied Hilbert_sort_middle_2.h. Median is measured in the original executable and checked in both executables during instrumentation validation.

Each trial calls spatial_sort with its default parameters and inserts the resulting vector point by point, using a face of the previous vertex as the location hint. Wall and CPU clocks delimit sorting, insertion and the contiguous total. Reading, projection, input generation, sampling, copying, warm-up, path measurement, validation and destruction are outside the timed interval. The open path sums consecutive Euclidean distances in the actual insertion vector. The CPU clock query at the phase boundary contributes a small overhead to the insertion interval. Peak resident memory is measured for the whole policy process, including input preparation and validation, and is shared by its technical trials.

There are eight synthetic families. Uniform, clustered, concentrated, grid, duplicates and graded use 2^14, 2^16, 2^18 and 2^20 points. Uniform and clustered also use 2^22. The distinct collinear and exponential controls use 4096 and 16384 points: CGAL's one-dimensional location routine ignores the hint and scans finite edges. The graded family is two-dimensional and combines forty coordinate scales. Duplicates has three distinct collinear locations. The generator in bench.cpp defines all distributions exactly.

The four real datasets are DIMACS New York, California/Nevada and western-US road coordinates, and GSHHG 2.3.7 vertices within longitude [-25,45] and latitude [30,72]. These are four datasets from two public databases; the California/Nevada set overlaps the western-US set. Spherical Lambert azimuthal equal-area projections produce planar coordinates in metres. Repeated coordinates are retained. Each dataset is measured in full and at 2^16, 2^18, 2^20 and 2^22 when smaller than the full set. The seed defines a shuffled full dataset and nested prefixes. Source URLs, projection parameters, point counts, licenses and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in datasets.json.

Each of the 46 distribution-size cases uses seeds 1729 through 1735 with identical inputs across policies. There are five technical trials up to 65536 points, three up to 262144 and one above that size. The distinct collinear and exponential controls always use one trial per seed. The frozen schedule has 966 policy processes and 2730 timed constructions. Blocks and policy order are randomized with seed 8297361. Each process has a 4096-point uniform warm-up, fixed affinity to one CPU, an 8 GiB virtual-address limit and a 600-second process limit. Runs are serial. Failures and incomplete comparisons remain in the records.

An assertion-enabled ASan/UBSan build records the sequence inside the insertion loop. Python independently sums the path and counts distinct points. The 32 validation configurations cover all eight synthetic families, both builds and both sorting policies, with two trials per configuration. Production trials check triangulation validity, exact distinct-point counts, input preservation and phase-time sums. Paired comparisons verify identical input multisets and permutations. The expected effective dimension is one for collinear, exponential and duplicates, and two for every other dataset.

Analysis first takes the median of technical trials within each seed. Comparisons report geometric means of seven paired ratios and individual 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals using 20000 resamples. There is no multiplicity correction. These intervals describe the measured seeds and orders in the recorded environment. The timing breakdown supports a comparison of phases and an association with insertion-path length; it does not isolate point location, topological updates or cache effects.
